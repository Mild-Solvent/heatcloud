The plant, end to end

Schematic of the repository's system with this year's energy on each line, from the scenario and site chosen on the left. Hover a component for its role and the equation the model uses.

1

Data centre: the heat source

IT power for every hour of the year (rows are days, columns are hours). All of it ends up as heat; the recovered share goes to the heat exchanger.

Qsource = PIT × recovery  ·  PDC = PIT × PUE
2

Heat exchanger: crossing between loops

Keeps server coolant separate from the heat-pump circuit. It passes 97 % of the recovered heat up to its rating and costs 3 K of temperature.

QHX = min(ε·Qsource, QHX,max)  ·  Tevap = Tsource − ΔTapproach
3

Heat pump: lifting the temperature

COP from 45 % of the Carnot limit for each source temperature, against network supply temperature. Dots are certified Carrier points (Eurovent, 10 °C source) the repository used as a check.

COP = 0.45 · Tsink / (Tsink − Tevap), 2.2…7  ·  Qout = QHX · COP / (COP − 1)
4

District network: weather compensation

Colder days raise both the town's demand and the water temperature the heat pump must reach. Shaded band: the outdoor temperatures this site actually sees.

f = clamp((16 − Tout)/28, 0, 1)  ·  Tsupply = 55 + 25f  ·  demand = (2 + 18f′)/0.94 MW
5

Thermal storage: shifting heat in time

Daily range of stored energy through the year. It charges when the heat pump makes more than the town needs and discharges when it falls short.

Et+1 = Et(1 − 10⁻⁴) + 0.95·Qcharge − Qdischarge/0.95  ·  ±5 MW limits
6

Swimming pool: where its heat goes

Heat loss of the 500 m³ pool at 27.5 °C water in a 29 °C hall, from the pool model's own loss equations. The cover mainly cuts evaporation.

ΔT = (Qin − Qloss)·Δt / (ρ·V·cp)  ·  thermostat 27.35 ↔ 27.65 °C
7

Controller: which mode the pool plant is in

The pool model's Stateflow chart picks one of five modes every 15 minutes. Bars show hours spent in each mode over the 7-day test, for every original test case.

Equipment the engineers specified

From the repository's equipment-selection and engineering-release work (23 Oct 2026). None of it is a vendor quotation yet; the status column says what is still missing.

How many heat-pump modules?

Extra gas the network burns per year when heat-pump output is capped. Above 10 MW almost nothing is gained; the engineers chose 6 + 1 spare Carrier 61XWH-ZE17 units (9.42 MW nominal).

Pool heat exchanger: what it is worth

20-year NPV at 6 % for the direct 150 kW pool exchanger, against the installed cost. It pays off only if installed for under about €103k.

If nobody takes the heat: IT cooling fault simulator

The data centre must reject all 10 MW on its own dry coolers when the pool and town take nothing. One cooler fails at minute 10 and a spare starts after a delay. Coolant above 40 °C trips the servers. This is the repository's Simulink fault model, rerun live; it reproduces their six saved cases exactly.

Sizing the 250 kW pilot

The same formulas the engineers used for the 10 MW plant, applied to our pilot pod. Screening numbers for a first talk with a designer.

Pool exchanger sizing

Counter-flow plate exchanger, 150 kW into pool water returning at 27.5 °C and rising 2 K. Warmer source water means a smaller, cheaper exchanger; below about 32 °C it cannot work at all.
